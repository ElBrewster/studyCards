- a coefficient is a number used to multiply a variable
- a number on its own is called a constant
- a polynomial is an expression consisting of indeterminates(variables) and coefficients, that involves only the operations of addition, subtraction, multiplication, and positive-integer powers of variables
  (so monomial, binomial & trinomial: 3xy^2, 5x-1, 3x + 5y^2 - 3)
- 'like terms' are terms whose variables(and their exponents) are the same. Coefficient can be different.
- surd: when we can't simplify a number to remove a square root then it is a surd. Surds have a decimal which goes on forever without repeating, and are 'irrational numbers'
- a logarithm answers the question: how many of 'one number' multiply together to make another number? 2 _ 2 _ 2 = 8, so we had to multiply 3 of the 2s to get 8, so the logarithm is 3: log2(8) = 3
- so a logarithm answers a question like this: 2? = 8
  2^3 = 8 ... log2(8) = 3
  the 'base' is 2 and the 'exponent' is 3
  so the logarithm answers the question: what exponent do we need (for one number to become another number)?
  A^x = y...logA(y) = x
